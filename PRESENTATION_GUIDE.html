A Causal Decision-Time Framework for Behavioral Bias Detection and Long-Horizon Portfolio Evaluation with Counterfactual Verifiable Intelligence

PART 0 — The 60-Second Version

Behavioural biases cost individual investors a large share of their lifetime wealth. Existing financial AI detects those biases after the fact, predicts the market rather than your portfolio, and explains itself in language only a data scientist can read.

We built a system that does three things instead. One: it detects the bias at the moment of the decision, and models it causally — not just correlationally. Two: it simulates what that specific decision does to this specific person's money over the next 10 to 30 years. Three: it explains the whole thing in a story an ordinary investor can read, in English or Hindi, without a single technical term.

Objectives 1, 2 and 3 are complete and tested. Objective 4 — a tamper-evident audit trail — is what remains.

PART 1 — Why This Project Exists

Four gaps in the literature, and what each one means in practice:

Gap The problem Our objective
1 Bias detection is retrospective and correlational Detect at decision-time, model causally
2 Predictions are market-level, not person-level Individual portfolio simulation over 10–30 years
3 Explanations are post-hoc and model-centric Decision-centric counterfactual narrative
4 Recommendations are unverifiable black boxes Tamper-evident, auditable decision trail

The through-line: a decision is detected (1), its consequences are simulated (2), the whole thing is explained to the person (3), and the record is made verifiable (4).

Why synthetic data

This is the question you will be asked, so answer it before it is asked:

  • Ground truth for biases is impossible in real data. We cannot read a real investor's mind and label "this was loss aversion". Synthetic generation gives us the label.
  • Counterfactuals do not exist in history. We cannot replay 2020 with the investor holding instead of selling. Simulation can.
  • 30-year futures have not happened yet.
  • Causal validation needs known structure. We know the true data-generating process, so we can check whether the causal model recovers it.

Real market data (NIFTY 50 via yfinance) is used for live market context in the dashboard, so the system is not purely synthetic at run time.

PART 2 — Objective 1: Causal Decision-Time Reasoning ✅

Design and implement a causal decision-time framework that detects behavioral biases and models cause-and-effect relationships beyond retrospective correlational analysis.

What we built

A real-time classifier that, given a decision moment, names the behavioural bias at play — and a causal layer that estimates how much each factor actually pushed the decision.

Why this way

A classifier alone would only tell us what pattern matched. It could not answer "what if this person were less loss-averse?" — which is the question an intervention needs. So the classifier is paired with a causal model.

How — the pipeline

  1. Feature engineering — market context, position state, and personal profile combined into one decision-moment feature vector.
  2. Class imbalance handled with SMOTE on the training split only.
  3. XGBoost multi-class classifier → four classes: loss_aversion, herd, disposition, rational.
  4. Causal structure learning with a deliberate three-tier fallback: NOTEARS → Hill-Climb search → domain-knowledge graph. The shipped artefact is the domain-knowledge + correlation graph: 8 nodes, 12 edges, validated as a DAG.
  5. Effect estimation with DoWhy — backdoor.linear_regression on each treatment.
  6. Intervention simulation — what happens to sell-probability if we change one thing.

Tools used

XGBoost · scikit-learn · imbalanced-learn (SMOTE) · DoWhy · CausalNex / NOTEARS · pgmpy (Hill-Climb) · NetworkX · SHAP · FastAPI · yfinance

PART 3 — Objective 2: Individual, Portfolio-Aware Long-Horizon Evaluation ✅

Develop a personalized portfolio-level simulation engine to evaluate the long-term (10–30 years) impact of financial decisions under uncertainty and compounding effects.

What we built

A Monte Carlo engine that takes one person's portfolio and simulates two futures — holding versus selling — across their real investment horizon.

Why this way

A single-point prediction ("you'll have ₹X") would be dishonest; markets are uncertain. A distribution over thousands of futures tells the truth: here is the typical case, here is a bad case, here is a good one.

How — the mechanics (know these cold, you will be asked)

Personalised asset allocation:

base = 0.40 + (risk_tolerance × 0.50)
   age > 60  → × 0.60      age < 30  → × 1.10
   horizon > 20 → × 1.05   horizon < 10 → × 0.90
   clipped to [0.30, 0.90]

The HOLD path: current wealth × (1 + unrealised P&L), invested at the personalised allocation.

The SELL path — three separate penalties: 1. Realise 95% of the gain/loss 2. Pay ~5% transaction and impact cost 3. Reinvest at 30% of the original stock allocation — a frightened investor becomes 70% more conservative, and the money then compounds near the bond rate (5.5%)

Each year: wealth × (1 + allocation × market_return + (1 − allocation) × bond_return), sampling from 10,000 pre-generated 30-year market paths with crashes injected at years 10 and 20.

Say this: "Point 3 is the finding that surprised us. Selling isn't just leaving one investment — people put the money somewhere safe and leave it there. That reallocation, not the transaction cost, is what actually destroys long-term wealth."

Research on thousands of investors shows a loss feels about twice as painful as the same-sized gain feels good. But selling doesn't stop the loss — it makes it permanent."

Why templates, not an LLM

This is the design decision to defend. The rupee figures in these explanations drive real money decisions. A template physically cannot invent a number. An LLM can. So: - Deterministic Jinja2 templates are the authoritative path — always run, fully reproducible. - An optional LLM layer may improve wording only, behind a numeric guard: every number and rupee amount in the polished text must already exist in the template text, or the polish is discarded. Hallucinated figures are structurally unable to reach the user. - The system runs fully offline with the LLM off — which is how we demo it.

PART 5 — Next Scope: Objective 4

Implement a tamper-proof audit mechanism that records AI recommendations, causal assumptions, and counterfactual analyses to ensure transparency, accountability, and verifiable decision trails.

Where it stands today — be honest about this

Already built Missing
SHA-256 hashing of input / output / explanation No hash chain — previous_hash does not exist
Append-only audit_logs table No compliance query engine
Per-record tamper detection No counterfactual outcome verification
GET /audit/{decision_id} No capture of what the user actually did

The honest weakness: records currently self-verify in isolation. Deleting a whole record is undetectable, because nothing links one record to the next.

The plan

  1. DecisionBlock — block ID, timestamp, user, decision payload, previous_hash, own hash.
  2. BlockchainAuditSystem — genesis block, add_block(), verify_chain() that detects both edits and deletions, JSON persistence.
  3. Schema migration — add previous_hash, block_index, decision_payload, user_action, followed_advice, guarded so the existing database survives.
  4. verify_counterfactual_accuracy() — compare what we predicted against what actually happened, reusing the Objective 2 validator.
  5. AuditQueryEngine — "all loss-aversion cases in Q1", "cases where the user ignored advice".
  6. Compliance report — chain integrity, advice-followed rate, counterfactual accuracy.

Why Objective 3 makes this easy: the explainer already returns a flat, JSON-serialisable facts dictionary. That is exactly what a block hashes. No rework needed.

Beyond Objective 4 — future work

  • Quantified causal statements in the narrative ("raised your sell probability by 4 points")
  • Validation with real investors (readability study)
  • More Indian languages — the template architecture already supports it
  • Fine-tuning a small model on template output (noted, not recommended: it would train on our own output and be less verifiable)

PART 8 — LIKELY QUESTIONS

"Why synthetic data? Isn't that a weakness?"

Ground-truth bias labels are impossible in real data — we can't read minds. Counterfactuals don't exist in history. And the causal model can only be validated if we know the true structure. Live NIFTY data is used for market context at run time.

"Why not just use ChatGPT for the explanations?"

Because these explanations contain rupee figures that drive real decisions, and a language model can invent a number. Our templates cannot. We do offer an optional AI wording layer, but behind a numeric guard that discards any output containing a figure not in the source. We demonstrated it with an invalid key: the user still got a complete explanation with no error.

"Is 84% accuracy good enough?"

For four-class behavioural classification with cross-validated stability of ±0.01, yes. Herd detection is at 0.97 F1 and generalises perfectly on held-out tests. The weakest class is disposition at 0.75, which is expected — the disposition effect is only visible across many decisions, and the model sees one at a time.

"What if the model is wrong?"

Two safeguards. The explanation always states its confidence honestly — it says "we're fairly sure", never "certain". And the recommendation comes from the simulation, not the classifier, so a misread bias changes the framing, not the financial advice.

"How do you know the explanations are actually understandable?"

We measure it. Flesch Reading Ease 78.3 against a target of 60, plus automated completeness, actionability and personalisation checks, and a jargon blocklist enforced by tests. Sixty-four explanations evaluated. The honest gap is that we have not yet run a study with real readers.

"Why is there no Hindi PDF?"

ReportLab has no OpenType text shaper. Devanagari needs one — in कि the matra is stored after the consonant but must be drawn before it. Without shaping the PDF would be subtly wrong, so we refuse it and offer an HTML report that prints to a correct PDF from any browser.

"What's left?"

Objective 4. Hashing and an append-only audit table exist; the hash chain does not, so deleting a record is currently undetectable. That is the next build, and Objective 3's serialisable facts dictionary is already the right input for it.
